# Build Kitchen Rush

Create a polished Overcooked-inspired cooperative 3D cooking game for partyplay. ID kitchen-rush; title Kitchen Rush. Follow MANAGER.md here for ownership, coordination, research and shared API rules.

Target 2–10 phone controllers with a watching shared display and a fixed/isometric kitchen camera. Make 2–4 chefs feel excellent, and design larger layouts/workload scaling for 5–10 rather than cramming ten bodies into a four-player map. Render the kitchen only on the display; phones have ergonomic movement and context-sensitive pick-up/drop/interact/throw/dash controls with clear held-item and nearby-station feedback. Keyboard controls are useful for accessibility and QA. Use the implemented 60 Hz simulation, 20 Hz held inputs/snapshots and scene readiness, with server-owned rules.

Required core loop: receive ordered tickets → obtain ingredients → chop/prepare → cook with doneness/burning risk → assemble on a clean plate → serve matching order → earn score/tips/stars → wash returned dishes. Include accessible disposal/fire recovery, dropped items and sensible interactions when two chefs reach one item/station. Recipes and visuals must make the next step clear. Passing/throwing ingredients and dashing should create teamwork without unreliable selection or permanent blocking.

Build one excellent complete kitchen first, then at least four distinct, polished kitchens with different teamwork patterns: a welcoming open diner, split preparation/cooking space with a serving/pass route, a moving/conveyor or periodically changing bridge kitchen, and a demanding finale with a clearly telegraphed original environmental twist. Keep all essential stations reachable or provide alternate work during changes. Use several recognizable recipe families (e.g. salads, soups, burgers and pizza), introduced gradually. Each level needs readable goals, genuine difficulty progression, a complete 3–5-minute service, scores/star thresholds adjusted for roster and level, and replay/next-kitchen progression. Include a relaxed/practice setting; never fabricate submitted orders or treat a zero-action timeout as acceptance.

Art direction: charming toy-like 3D kitchen dioramas, original animated chefs with visible hands/held items and color-plus-number identity, tiled floors, rounded counters, steam/sizzle, clear progress indicators, and an order-ticket HUD that remains readable with ten players. Provide satisfying pickup/chop/cook/serve feedback and short celebrations, respecting reduced motion. Design is judged in motion during service, not just from the lobby. Music is reserved for the user; do not add tracks.

Authoritative rules must cover collision/navigation, item ownership and conservation, station occupancy, progress and interruption, correct/incorrect plating, burn/fire/extinguish behavior, serving exactly once, ticket ordering/expiry, tips, dish return/washing, disconnected held items, ties and clear completion. Avoid arbitrary reliable-action exhaustion in a long busy service; propose a bounded shared protocol extension to the manager if needed. Never increase shared limits or fork the room transport yourself.

Write DESIGN.md with coordinates, camera/occlusion, interaction targeting, workload scaling, recipes, level topology and budgets. Add meaningful rule tests, actual UI-to-orders-to-score-to-results browser tests at minimum and maximum rosters, replay, reconnect while holding/cooking, two chefs contending for a station, A → other game → A, and the shared viewport checks. Record how many real orders were served, exact build, screenshots inspected and performance. Complete README and QA report. Build a game people can play repeatedly, not a kitchen scene or disconnected collection of buttons.

## User scope clarification — 2026-09-08

Professional quality and sustained play are acceptance requirements. Build a level progression where each stage introduces a new mechanic, challenge, functionality, or purposeful combination. Target 8–12 authored stages with visible progression and retained stars/unlocks; four recolored layouts do not satisfy this. Introduce recipes/processes and logistics gradually, add distinct spatial and timed dynamic challenges, then combine learned mechanics in advanced stages. Each level needs a complete playable service, reachable stations at advertised roster sizes, fair progression, scoring and replay. The initial four-kitchen build is a vertical slice for controls/rendering QA, not the finish line.
